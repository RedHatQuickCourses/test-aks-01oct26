In the modern enterprise landscape, SAP environments are no longer confined to static, on-premises data centers. To support the "Intelligent Enterprise," organizations must adopt a flexible, scalable, and resilient infrastructure. Red Hat’s Open Hybrid Cloud strategy provides a consistent foundation for SAP workloads, allowing businesses to move seamlessly between on-premises environments and public cloud providers while maintaining operational consistency.

Red Hat enables SAP customers to deploy a unified, automated, and end-to-end infrastructure. Whether you are performing a greenfield deployment of SAP S/4HANA or migrating legacy systems, this strategy ensures that your "Digital Core"—which includes SAP S/4HANA, BW/4HANA, and SAP HANA databases—operates with the same reliability and management standards across every footprint.

There are four key components to this strategy. First is operational consistency, which allows you to use the same Red Hat Enterprise Linux foundation across on-premises servers, private clouds, and major public cloud providers like AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud. Second is automation, where you utilize Red Hat Ansible Automation Platform to standardize installation, configuration, and maintenance, reducing human error and deployment time. Third is scalability, allowing you to rapidly provision infrastructure to match business growth or seasonal peaks in demand. Finally, high availability ensures that your critical SAP business processes remain online through enterprise-grade clustering, even in the event of hardware or software failure.

Now, let’s look at how to plan a multi-cloud SAP deployment. Before you begin, ensure you have access to the Red Hat Customer Portal, the SAP Service Marketplace for your binaries, and a solid understanding of your organization's compliance requirements regarding data locality.

The first step is an inventory assessment. Categorize your SAP components into core systems, such as S/4HANA, which typically require high-performance, low-latency, and high-availability setups, and modern cloud-native applications, such as IoT or business networks, which may run on containers.

Second, select your infrastructure footprint. Define where your systems will reside. For a true hybrid strategy, you can deploy Red Hat Enterprise Linux for SAP Solutions on-premises to leverage existing hardware, or utilize the pre-configured, SAP-certified images available in public cloud marketplaces to ensure full support and performance optimization.

Third, standardize your deployment using automation. Create a baseline using Ansible Playbooks to ensure every instance has the necessary tuning for SAP HANA. You should ensure that tuned profiles, such as the `sap-hana` profile, are applied, and verify that all required kernel parameters are set to SAP-compliant values. To apply the SAP HANA tuned profile to your instance, you would run the command: `tuned-adm profile sap-hana`.

Finally, step four is verification. Confirm that your hybrid footprint is reporting correctly to your management consoles, such as Red Hat Insights for RHEL. This ensures you maintain full visibility into the health and security of your SAP estate, regardless of whether it is running on-premises or in the cloud.

If you encounter issues, here is some troubleshooting guidance. If you experience consistency issues where an SAP system behaves differently in the cloud compared to on-premises, verify that you are using the same Red Hat Enterprise Linux for SAP Solutions repositories. Cloud-specific repositories often contain specialized drivers and optimization packages required for SAP performance. 

Additionally, regarding high availability, ensure that your cluster fencing, or STONITH mechanisms, are configured according to the specific requirements of your cloud provider. These configurations differ significantly from traditional hardware-based IPMI or ILO fencing, so take care to follow the provider-specific documentation.

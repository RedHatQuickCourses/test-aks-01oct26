Running SAP HANA on Red Hat Enterprise Linux, or RHEL, requires strict adherence to certified configurations to ensure performance, stability, and supportability. This session outlines the architectural requirements and foundational considerations for deploying SAP HANA workloads.

Before you begin a deployment, it is critical to align your infrastructure with both Red Hat and SAP guidelines. Please keep in mind that in official documentation, the term RHEL serves as a synonym for both RHEL for SAP HANA and RHEL for SAP Solutions. You should always consult the official SAP HANA Hardware Directory to ensure your hardware choice is validated for your specific workload.

When it comes to hardware certification, SAP HANA requires specific profiles to handle high-memory throughput and low-latency storage. First, you must use servers, storage systems, and cloud instance types that are explicitly listed in the SAP HANA Hardware Directory. Second, for non-appliance scenarios, verify that your server model is compatible with your specific RHEL release by checking the Red Hat Hardware Compatibility List.

Regarding deployment models, your requirements will differ based on the scale. You might implement a scale-up model, which is a single-node environment where the database is contained within one server, or a scale-out model, which is a distributed environment where the database is partitioned across multiple nodes. For both models, if you are implementing high availability, you must utilize the RHEL High Availability Add-On. Support for these environments follows the specific policies defined in the Red Hat support policies for RHEL High Availability clusters.

Now, let's walk through an activity to verify your system readiness. To complete this, you will need an active RHEL for SAP Solutions subscription and root access to your target server.

First, check your subscription type. You need to ensure your system is registered with the correct subscription, as this provides access to the necessary repositories for SAP HANA. You can do this by running the command: `subscription-manager list --consumed`.

Second, validate your hardware certification. You can retrieve your hardware vendor and product information to compare against the SAP HANA Hardware Directory by running: `dmidecode -t system | grep -E "Manufacturer|Product"`.

Third, check your repository availability. Confirm that the SAP-specific repositories are enabled to access the packages required for SAP HANA optimizations. Run the command: `yum repolist | grep sap`. If you find that these repositories are missing, you can enable the required channels by using the `subscription-manager` command to enable the repository for your specific RHEL version.

To wrap up, here are a few architectural best practices. Always leverage the Update Services for SAP Solutions, often referred to as E4S. This allows you to stay on a specific RHEL minor release for an extended period, which is crucial for effective lifecycle management of SAP HANA environments. 

Finally, regarding high availability, ensure that your fencing mechanisms and resource agents are configured according to the official Red Hat documentation for SAP HANA clusters. This is essential to prevent data corruption during failover events.
